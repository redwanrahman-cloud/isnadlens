# Automatic source identification

Auto mode helps users who do not know whether a quotation is Quran or Hadith. It identifies direct quotation candidates in admitted editions and then runs the existing selected-source verification pipeline. It does not infer authenticity from search popularity or website appearance.

Explicit attribution is labelled **source stated in your claim**, not a verified quotation match. A claimed Quran reference is still checked against Quran; the tool does not silently rewrite a mistaken attribution. Unattributed text can match an unchanged display/search edition exactly or through query-only Arabic spelling/diacritic variants. Such matches are labelled accordingly. Paraphrases without a direct match ask for manual source selection.

When a Quran verse is also quoted inside admitted Hadith text, both families are reported and the user chooses the intended source. Candidate displays are bounded while classification considers all matching candidates. A missing match is not proof that a quotation is absent from all Islamic sources, fabricated, or weak.

Hadith grades and takhrij are the publisher's unchanged fields with a source link. The publisher distinguishes several detailed categories, including authentic/good chains and cases without a stated judgment; these are not flattened into a model-created strong/weak score. For example, the official [intentions record](https://hadeethenc.com/en/browse/hadith/4560) supplies its own grade and reference. The app does not independently grade a narration. Website/source integrity, faithful quotation and the publisher's Hadith grade remain separate.

Normal weather/greeting questions and personal/privacy refusals remain outside textual assessment. No-match and ambiguous outcomes do not call the semantic provider. Display-language switching preserves source text, record identity and sealed evidence.
